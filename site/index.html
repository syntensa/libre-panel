New in 0.5: video mode, cards, notices and an app of its own What's new

Your PC's vitals as moving light.

Libre Panel drives the small USB system-monitor panels sold as TURZX, Turing Smart Screen, WeAct, Kipye and more: your processor, graphics card, fans, network, what is playing and the weather, at up to 50 frames a second. It works out of the box: the downloads carry what the features run, and the setup does the rest after one question. Free and open source, for Windows and Linux, without an account or a cloud.

A 30-second tour

A panel that moves with your PC

Every clip here is Libre Panel itself, recorded frame by frame with demo values: Lumen on the 9.2" bar, Studio for the notice, Encore, Nimbus and Almanac, Libre Default on a 3.5" panel, and the panel's own start screen as Libre Panel draws it.

Video mode

Up to 50 frames a second

TURZX USB panels have a video decoder, and Libre Panel feeds it a frame every 20 milliseconds. Numbers count to each new reading, rings and bars glide, graphs scroll a little on every frame, and fans turn at their real speed.

The downloads carry the ffmpeg it needs, and it turns itself on on the 9.2". Panels without a decoder get still frames, sent where something changed.

The look

Values drawn as light

Rings, bars and graphs sit as light in a milled groove, lettering as faceted metal or lit acrylic. One light for the whole panel glints on the frames and sweeps across on the hour, and fine dust drifts in the depth behind.

Each finish is one click in Look: light, facet, acrylic or flat.

Cards

Cards that change by themselves

What is playing, with its cover and its progress; when nothing plays, the weather with moving symbols, the next hours and the rain of the next two hours; when there is neither, the day. A line of light ignites between two cards: one rolls up into it, the next rolls down.

With the downloads service on, the network card shows a download in its place while one runs.

Notices

A band, or a card

Turn the volume and a band rolls down from a glowing edge, its number counting while you turn. A new track with its cover, a sensor past a limit you set, a stick or a game controller plugged in: short notices in the colours of the theme.

The notices and devices services are off until you switch them on; devices is for Windows.

Game mode

An entrance for every game

Libre Panel finds the game you start, from a list of your own or your Steam library. The screen closes to a line, the game's name lights up, and the game screen opens with the frame rate, its 1% low and who holds it back. After a round, an optional report compares it with your last ten.

The frame rate comes from Intel's PresentMon (Windows), which the download carries.

Screens

For music, the weather and the time

Encore puts the cover large and its colours behind the whole screen, the time with rolling digits beside it and the volume as levels that rise while you turn it. Nimbus has the weather now, the next hours and days, the moon as it is lit tonight and a small clock. Almanac is the time beside the month; Almanac Analog puts it on a face whose second hand glides.

Made for the 8.8" and 9.2" bars, and laid out anew for every other panel. Switched on in Design → Pages, Encore takes over while music plays and Almanac when you step away.

Transitions

The next screen opens in a line of light

One theme for the desk, another while music plays, a third at night: the autopilot picks the theme by rules you write, and a line ignites at mid-height and opens onto the next screen, already live.

The autopilot is a service too, off until you switch it on.

Screens in turn

Five minutes this, ten minutes that, and round again

On Start, pick the screens, give each its seconds, minutes or hours, and the panel shows them one after another, each opening from a line of light, and from the top again after the last.

A game, a notice or a theme a service asks for still comes first.

Every panel

From 0.96 inches to 12.3

Every theme moves to every panel and lays itself out for it, round and portrait ones included. Libre Default, shown here on a 3.5" panel, is where every panel but the bars starts: lit rings, a history that scrolls and a small card in the corner.

Start screen

The panel's own start, in Libre Panel's look

TURZX USB panels show a logo of their own when they are switched on and play a clip when the PC starts, before any app talks to them. Libre Panel draws both for the size of your panel and puts them on it with one click, after a question.

Settings → Panel → Start screen. The factory logo stays on the panel and comes back with another click.

Fits every PC

Named after your hardware, with nothing left blank

  • The processor, graphics card and memory under the names they report, found by Libre Panel itself without administrator rights, or the names you type.
  • A part steps aside when its readings are missing: the disk takes the ring of a graphics card that reports nothing.
  • Nothing playing and no weather: the card shows the day.
  • Dates and weather in English or German, for the place you set.

Out of the box

Everything it needs comes with it

Sensors, fans, graphics card, game frame rates (Windows) and the 50 fps video mode need no other program. The downloads carry what the features run, in a tools folder: ffmpeg for the video mode, and on Windows PresentMon and the installer of the PawnIO driver. One setup step does the rest.

Sensors

Read only while a theme or a service shows them.

  • LibreHardwareMonitor, built inWindows GPU, temperatures, power and fans, read inside Libre Panel with no other program. The sensor service adds the CPU's temperature, power and clock, the mainboard's fans, and the memory and drives.
  • The kernel's own sensorsLinux Processor, graphics card (AMD, Intel, NVIDIA) and NVMe drives, read from the kernel and NVIDIA's driver: no extra program, no lm-sensors. The mainboard's fans need one click.
  • Load ten times a second CPU and GPU load as it happens, the GPU as Task Manager shows it, and the volume on every frame.
  • What is playing Title, cover and the player's icon, from Windows' media controls or any MPRIS player on Linux.
  • Weatheroff From Open-Meteo, with the next hours and the rain of the next two hours; no API key.
  • And more Every core, drive and fan, calendars, Home Assistant, MQTT, sunrise and the moon, and PresentMon for games.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your PC except the weather you turn on.

No account, no telemetry, no update check. The app is served to your own PC only, and only to requests that carry a secret token, new at every start.

  • The weatherOff until you set a place; then only its coordinates go to Open-Meteo, every 15 minutes. A place you look up by name is sent when you press Search, and kept to about a kilometre.
  • What you connect yourselfCalendars, Home Assistant and MQTT talk to the addresses you give them. The ping of the network details module connects to 1.1.1.1 while it is on the panel, and a cover a player names by a web address is loaded from there.
  • The optional servicesKeep what they learn on this PC, in a file you can open or delete. FanControl's configuration is read, never written. No track title, window title or file name is read.
  • Your hardware's namesNever go to the log, and the report of libre-panel doctor has no serial numbers and no personal paths.

Install

Install and first start

No Python needed. From the latest release on GitHub.

Windows 10 and 11

A setup for all users: a start menu entry, an optional start at login and three checked boxes that say what each is, the sensor service, the PawnIO driver and what game frame rates need. Windows asks once for administrator rights; Libre Panel itself runs without them. Install for me only is still possible. The uninstaller keeps your settings and themes. A portable zip too: its Unlock all sensors sets up the same.

Download the setup Not code-signed yet: SmartScreen asks once (More info → Run anyway).

Linux

An AppImage for x86-64: make it executable and start it, no installation. Start offers to let your user use the panel and to load the driver of the mainboard's fan chip, with one click and one password. A tarball too.

Download the AppImage The AppImage opens the app in the browser for now. Setup per system
  1. Close the vendor appIt keeps the panel to itself.
  2. Start Libre PanelWith the panel plugged in. A TURZX USB panel is found by itself; on Linux, Start offers the panel's permission with one click.
  3. Pick a lookStart asks "How should it look?" with the themes made for your panel.
  4. Turn on what you wantThe weather, video mode and the services in Settings; the tray icon has the rest.

With Python instead:

pipx install "libre-panel[usb,serial,tray] @ git+https://github.com/syntensa/libre-panel@v0.5.0"
libre-panel tray               # the background app with its tray icon
libre-panel doctor             # check your panel, write a report

FAQ

Questions

Which panel do I have?

The product name and the back label usually say it. Plugged in, libre-panel devices lists what it sees: a TURZX USB panel shows up with the USB id 1cbe:… and its size, the others as a virtual COM port. Start in the app names the panel it found, and Another panel lets you choose. When in doubt, libre-panel doctor asks and checks.

Why do the CPU temperature and power show -- on Windows?

The Windows downloads read the graphics card, power and fans with the LibreHardwareMonitor library built in. The processor's temperature, power and clock, the temperatures of memory and drives and the mainboard's fans also need administrator rights and the PawnIO driver, which the download carries. Libre Panel's sensor service has both, and the setup installs the two (the checked boxes Sensor service and PawnIO driver), so after the setup nothing is left to do.

You see -- only where that part was not set up: after Install for me only, or with the portable zip. Unlock all sensors on Start, or in Settings → Sensors, sets it up with one question of Windows, and Libre Panel itself goes on without administrator rights (how it is kept safe). With a pip install there is no service: install PawnIO and run libre-panel autostart enable --elevated in a terminal opened as administrator, but only where only administrators can change Libre Panel's files (why). A LibreHardwareMonitor you run yourself with its web server works too.

How do I get the smooth video mode? Do I need ffmpeg?

Video mode is for the TURZX USB panels, which have a video decoder. The Windows and Linux downloads carry the ffmpeg it needs, so you only name the panel's standby clip, as the vendor app shows it, in Settings → Panel. On the 9.2" it then turns itself on; on the other TURZX USB panels, set it to on to try it. With a pip install, install ffmpeg yourself (Windows: winget install ffmpeg; Linux: your distribution's package). Nothing is saved on the panel (details).

Do I need LibreHardwareMonitor, FanControl or PresentMon?

No other program is needed: LibreHardwareMonitor and Intel's PresentMon are in the Windows downloads. The Game frame rate source is on by default on Windows and counts a game's frames while a theme shows them. PresentMon needs administrator rights or your account in the Windows group "Performance Log Users", which the setup's third box (or Unlock all sensors) arranges; it applies from your next sign-in (how). The cooling analysis reads FanControl's fan curves, if you use FanControl.

Can it harm my panel?

Libre Panel sends display commands only: pictures, brightness, video. It never touches the firmware and stores nothing on the panel; firmware updates stay the vendor app's job.

Does it run on macOS?

Not since 0.5: Windows and Linux are supported, and nobody downloaded the macOS builds of earlier releases.

Where it started

It began as SPUR II, on one 9.2" panel

Libre Panel grew out of SPUR II, a private dashboard for one panel on one PC. SPUR II proved the panel's USB protocol and its video mode on real hardware, and its ideas (light in milled grooves, the cards, the band, the tube) are now Libre Panel's own, for every panel and every PC.

Its eight screens ship as the legacy collection SPUR II, at the end of Start → All.

Libre Panel is free software under the GPL-3.0. It stands on the shoulders of turing-smart-screen-python, whose reverse-engineering documents these panels. The downloads carry ffmpeg (GPL-3.0-or-later), the Windows ones also LibreHardwareMonitor (MPL-2.0), PresentMon (MIT) and the installer of the PawnIO driver; sources and licences are in NOTICE.md.
